THE THIRD WAY: HHGttG AND THE FIRST PROTOTYPE
==============================================

Status: Working system definition, version 0.2

1. THEORETICAL DEFINITION
-------------------------

The Third Way is the larger idea: an open, local-first form of personal
computing centered on the person rather than a platform owner. HHGttG remains
the protocol and architecture through which person-owned Decks communicate
with modular Interfacing Devices and federated Nodes.

The Third Way exists to give an individual practical access to knowledge, other
people, services, tools, and the digitally represented world without making a
central platform the owner of the person's identity or information.

The Third Way is not primarily:

* A smartphone.
* A single physical product.
* An artificial-intelligence chatbot.
* A social-media company.
* A cloud service.
* A blockchain.
* An application store.

It is a durable relationship among a person, their information, their chosen
computational agent, replaceable hardware, and an interoperable network.

The persistent platform is the person. Processors, displays, applications,
models, services, employers, and hosting providers are resources that the
person may attach, authorize, replace, or leave.

2. CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLE
---------------------------

Every person should be able to enter the digital noosphere through an
interface that serves them, protects their autonomy, helps them understand
what they encounter, and respects the equal autonomy of everyone else.

Consequences of this principle include:

* Identity originates under the person's cryptographic control.
* Personal information remains portable and available in documented formats.
* The agent's authority never exceeds machine-enforced permissions.
* Essential functions remain useful without continuous internet access.
* Advertising and sponsorship cannot secretly determine answers or priorities.
* No mandatory company, Node, blockchain, or identity provider controls entry.
* Hardware and software must not be configured to unreasonably harm or disrupt
  other people or systems.
* A person can change vendors, employers, hosts, interfaces, and hardware
  without abandoning their digital self.

3. THE TWO PRIMARY OBJECTS
--------------------------

THE DECK

A Deck is a person's trusted computing core. It contains or controls:

* Cryptographic identities and credentials.
* Private information and structured personal records.
* Applications and sandboxed packages.
* Personal agents and their authorized tools.
* Permissions and user modes.
* Device discovery and coordination.
* Communication with other Decks and Nodes.
* Local knowledge, search, task history, and audit records.

The Deck is the authority. Connecting a peripheral, cartridge, application, or
Node never grants it unrestricted control.

THE INTERFACING DEVICE

An Interfacing Device is any modular physical object that adds one or more
capabilities to a Deck:

* Input or output.
* Storage or packaged information.
* General or specialized computation.
* Sensors or physical actuation.
* Local or long-range communication.

Examples include a keyboard/display, earbud, camera, radio, drawing tablet,
MIDI controller, VR headset, storage cartridge, compute accelerator, and a game
controller containing the complete game.

Every capable Interfacing Device should declare what it provides, requests,
consumes, stores, and executes. The Deck negotiates capabilities and enforces
the resulting limits.

4. NODES AND THE PEOPLE'S NETWORK
---------------------------------

A Node is a locally operated shared network host. A household, company,
library, school, cooperative, municipality, or community may operate one.

A Node may provide:

* Shared files and project spaces.
* Messages, groups, and community directories.
* Replicated public knowledge.
* Backups and synchronization.
* Rendering, inference, search, or other pooled computation.
* Gateways between live networks and delayed physical delivery.

Nodes federate through public protocols. Commercial Nodes are permitted but
receive no architectural privilege. A large Node may consist of many machines
while presenting one governed service identity.

5. SOFTWARE ARCHITECTURE
------------------------

The reference implementation should initially use Linux and commodity
hardware. A custom operating-system kernel is not required to prove the idea.

The initial architecture consists of:

* A capability and permission broker.
* A device broker.
* Sandboxed portable application components.
* Native services only where drivers or real-time behavior require them.
* A shared Semiotic Engine broker rather than an unrestricted language model
  inside each package.
* A structured local database.
* Content-addressed storage for large or immutable objects.
* An append-only journal for changes, provenance, and synchronization.
* An event-driven task system with bounded resource declarations.
* Live networking plus store-and-forward delivery for interrupted connections.

Work is scheduled in four broad classes:

1. Real-time: audio, tracking, instruments, and immediate controls.
2. Interactive: typing, ordinary searches, messages, and interface actions.
3. Analytical: image interpretation, complex reasoning, and large searches.
4. Background: indexing, synchronization, backup, and maintenance.

Complex prototype tasks may take as long as five minutes. Speed must not take
precedence over informing the user of the results and implications of what
their question actually posits. The Deck should show progress, limitations,
and uncertainty rather than silently substituting a materially weaker task.

6. IDENTITY AND TRUST
---------------------

Identity is cryptographic, user-controlled, plural, recoverable, and usable
offline. A person may maintain separate personal, work, community, public,
pseudonymous, and temporary identities.

Blockchains and public registries are optional verification or transparency
layers. They are not the source or owner of identity.

The architecture must support key rotation, lost-device revocation, trusted
introductions, multiple devices, encrypted recovery material, and recovery
assistance from people or institutions selected by the owner.

7. DATA AND APPLICATION PORTABILITY
-----------------------------------

HHG packages may carry applications, knowledge, media, permissions, and
experiences independently of a particular vendor platform.

Examples include:

* A note written at home and delivered to a friend's Deck through a live or
  delayed message route.
* A portable music workstation connected to a MIDI keyboard and display.
* A spatial cartridge containing a VR room and its assets.
* A controller that contains a complete game and optionally the player's saves.
* An encrypted animation workspace taken between a company Node and home.
* A knowledge cartridge used by a simple voice-and-earbud cooking interface.

Possessing a cartridge does not automatically grant unrestricted authority.
Code remains sandboxed, permissions persist with protected information, and
personal changes can be stored as separate user-owned layers.

8. FIRST PHYSICAL PROTOTYPE
---------------------------

The selected first Deck is an Anbernic RG35XX H handheld containing:

* A 1.5 GHz quad-core 64-bit Arm Cortex-A53 processor.
* 1 GB of memory.
* Two removable microSD slots.
* A 3.5-inch 640 by 480 display.
* Integrated directional, action, shoulder, and analog controls.
* 2.4 and 5 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 4.2.
* A rechargeable battery and portable enclosure.
* A Linux-based operating environment.

This replaces the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W as the first active prototype target.
The Pi remains a useful deferred hardware-adaptation experiment.

The RG35XX H integrates storage, controls, display, battery, and networking in
one inexpensive object. That lets the first work concentrate on Guide behavior
and interoperability rather than assembling a display, keyboard, power system,
and enclosure.

The hardware is still below the intended capacity for a useful general-purpose
local Semiotic Engine. It should run the Deck runtime and delegate optional
Semiotic Engine work to an authorized Node without surrendering identity,
permission, or execution authority.

9. FIRST USER INTERFACE
-----------------------

The interface is keyboard-first and usable without a mouse. Its initial home
view should expose a small number of stable functions:

    GUIDE

    [1] Ask
    [2] Notes
    [3] Messages
    [4] Files
    [5] Devices
    [6] Tasks

    Node status: Offline
    Active tasks: None

A dedicated Guide command should allow the person to express an intention from
anywhere. Conventional menus remain available so the agent is never the only
way to operate the Deck.

The interface should progressively disclose detail: a plain explanation first,
then sources, permissions, logs, technical information, and developer controls
when requested.

10. PROTOTYPE SOFTWARE SCOPE
----------------------------

The prototype should prove the architecture rather than imitate every feature
of a modern phone. Its minimum software functions are:

* Create and unlock a local user identity.
* Create, edit, organize, and search notes.
* Import and search a limited document collection.
* Ask a local agent questions grounded in authorized local information.
* Display sources, uncertainty, task status, and relevant actions.
* Normalize the handheld's integrated controls and display, and represent a
  discovered external receiver through its advertised capabilities.
* Enforce explicit capabilities between the agent, data, and devices.
* Remain useful with its internet connection disabled.
* Preserve data safely across shutdown and unexpected interface removal.

The next networking milestone is secure communication between two independently
administered systems without requiring an account from a central provider.

11. FIRST PROTOTYPE ACCEPTANCE TEST
-----------------------------------

The first acceptance test is continuity from a Node to the portable Deck:

1. Play an authorized local video on the user's desktop Node.
2. Pause it and request "Continue on Deck."
3. Connect the Anbernic Deck to the Node through USB.
4. Confirm the destination, available space, filename, file size, and current
   playback position before writing.
5. Transfer the video and a small, open resume record to temporary storage on
   the Deck.
6. Verify the complete file, then make both items visible to the Deck as one
   atomic handoff; an interrupted copy must not appear complete.
7. Disconnect the Deck and resume the video locally within two seconds of the
   position reported by the Node, allowing for video keyframe seeking.
8. Require no internet connection, cloud account, or Semiotic Engine.

The test applies to user-owned, public-domain, or otherwise authorized local
media. It does not claim to extract or copy protected subscription streams.

The following acceptance test is local media handoff in an unfamiliar home:

1. Store a user-owned movie on the Deck's removable memory.
2. Join the same Wi-Fi network as an unknown-model smart television.
3. Discover which standard local playback capabilities the television offers.
4. Offer only the selected movie through a temporary, bounded media share.
5. Ask the user to select and confirm the destination television.
6. Cause the television to retrieve and play the movie without uploading it to
   an internet service.
7. Show whether discovery, authorization, transfer, decoding, and playback have
   succeeded or failed without claiming capabilities the television lacks.
8. Withdraw the temporary share when playback ends, the Deck leaves, or the
   user revokes it.

This test demonstrates the Third Way's central claim at a small but visible scale:
the person carries data and intent, while unfamiliar local technology supplies
a capability through an interoperable interface.


12. SECONDARY EVENT-ROUTING TEST
--------------------------------

`MESSAGE-CHIME-0` tests a simple Guide Recipe across a phone and Deck:

WHEN my phone receives a text from Dad
THEN ask my Deck to play the selected sound once.

The sender's and recipient's cellular providers are outside the Guide path and
do not need to match. The phone observes the receipt through an explicitly
authorized operating-system adapter, reduces it to the minimum event needed by
the Recipe, and sends that event to the paired Deck on the local Wi-Fi network.
The default event contains a paired-person identifier, a unique event ID, and a
time—not the message text.

The Deck accepts events only from the paired phone, rejects old or repeated
event IDs, limits repeated sound triggers, and requires separate
`messages.observe-sender` and `audio.play` capabilities. The sound is stored and
played on the Deck. Platform-specific phone restrictions must be reported
plainly rather than bypassed.
