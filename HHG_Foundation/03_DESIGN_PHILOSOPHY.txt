HHGttG DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
========================

Status: Foundational summary, version 0.2

THE PERSON IS THE PLATFORM

A person's identity, information, relationships, preferences, and agent should
outlast any particular screen, processor, employer, application, or hosting
provider. Technology attaches to the person through their Deck, not the person
to a vendor's platform.

SERVE INTENTION, NOT ENGAGEMENT

The Guide exists to help a person understand something, communicate, create,
organize, decide, or act. It should not optimize for advertising exposure,
compulsive use, time-on-screen, or dependence on the system.

INCREASE HUMAN AGENCY

Agentic assistance should translate human intentions into understandable plans
and actions. It must not quietly replace the person's judgment or acquire the
person's authority. Observation, interpretation, preparation, action, and
delegation require progressively stronger permission.

PROTOCOLS BEFORE PLATFORMS

People using different Decks, Nodes, interfaces, and implementations should
still communicate and exchange information. No central service should own the
social graph, identity system, application channel, or network itself.

SIMPLE ON THE SURFACE, INSPECTABLE UNDERNEATH

Ordinary use should require little technical knowledge. A person who wants to
understand more should be able to inspect sources, actions, permissions,
provenance, network behavior, and technical details. Simplicity must not depend
on secrecy.

CREATION IS ORDINARY USE

Making a small Guide program should be a normal creative activity, not a
specialist privilege. A person who uses computers regularly should be able to
connect an input to an action, test it safely, understand what went wrong, and
share the result with a friend without first studying conventional software
engineering.

Guide development tools, Recipes, terminology, messages, and documentation
must begin in plain language. Necessary technical terms must be introduced
where they become useful, defined immediately, and connected to a concrete
example. This requirement does not permit technical facts, risks, or limits to
be hidden. The design must offer a short path for common work and a visible
path down to exact implementation details.

MODULARITY WITHOUT BURDEN

People should be able to choose displays, controls, sensors, radios, storage,
and compute appropriate to their bodies, resources, and purposes. A standard
preassembled configuration should remain easy for someone who does not want to
manage modules.

LATERAL THINKING WITH WITHERED TECHNOLOGY

HHG adopts Gunpei Yokoi's principle of "Lateral Thinking with Withered
Technology": use mature, inexpensive, abundant, and well-understood technology
in new combinations instead of making novelty or maximum performance an end in
itself.

The Guide's innovation should come primarily from the relationships it creates
among familiar components. Commodity processors, cheap displays, established
radios, standard connectors, discarded tablets, and ordinary storage can
become a new personal and community computing environment when joined by an
open capability protocol.

"Withered" means seasoned and dependable, not insecure or unsupported.
Obsolete components may be safely repurposed only within capabilities their
condition can support. Security primitives, exposed network software, and
safety-critical controls must remain maintained and replaceable. Cutting-edge
technology is appropriate when it solves a demonstrated human need, but it
should enter as an optional capability rather than make the entire network
dependent upon scarcity, expense, or one vendor.

LOCAL FIRST; NETWORK CAPABLE

The Deck should retain useful knowledge and essential functions when isolated.
Connectivity expands its reach but does not grant a remote service ownership of
the person. Intermittent links and physically carried information are normal
operating conditions.

HONESTNESS BEFORE SPEED

Fast responses are desirable, but speed must not take precedence over informing
the user of the results and implications of what their question posits. The
Guide should communicate progress, evidence, limits, and uncertainty plainly.

AUTONOMY IS RECIPROCAL

The Guide protects its owner while respecting the equal autonomy of others. A
Deck must resist unauthorized access and must not use its hardware, software,
agents, or network capacity to unreasonably harm, deceive, surveil, overwhelm,
or disrupt other people and systems.

ACCESSIBILITY IS ARCHITECTURAL

Text, speech, touch, physical controls, assistive devices, and agent interaction
should operate on the same underlying information and capabilities. No single
interface is assumed to suit every person.

KNOWLEDGE SHOULD REMAIN EXAMINABLE

The Guide should help people reach humanity's accumulated knowledge while
preserving sources, provenance, competing interpretations, and uncertainty. An
agent should help the person understand information rather than become an
unquestionable authority standing between the person and it.

DESIGN FOR DECADES, FAILURE, AND EXIT

Hardware breaks, companies fail, communities change, and formats age. Identity
must be recoverable; data must be exportable; old formats must be convertible;
and providers must be replaceable. A person must be able to leave a vendor,
employer, or Node without leaving behind their digital self.

OPENNESS MUST RESIST CAPTURE

Corporations may build hardware, operate Nodes, sell services, and contribute
software, but money cannot purchase architectural privilege. Open specifications,
public tests, reciprocal licensing, transparent governance, portable data, and
independent implementations must keep HHG centered on individuals and
communities.

GUIDING QUESTION

Every major decision should be tested by asking:

    Does this help a person understand and direct their digital life while
    preserving the same freedom and safety for everyone else?

If the answer is no, the design does not belong in the Guide.
